AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Johnstown, PA

Repair and drying after a clogged condensate drain, a rusted pan or a frozen coil leaks into your Johnstown home.

☎ (814) 358-8169

Where AC leak water comes from

Air conditioning removes moisture from the air, and that water leaves the system through a condensate drain line. When the line clogs, the drain pan rusts through or the evaporator coil freezes and then thaws, the water overflows into the ceiling, closet or wall near the unit. AC leak water damage repair is the cleanup and drying after that happens, and it starts by stopping the unit so it stops making more water.

Turn the thermostat to off. Then put a bucket or towels where the water is falling, and call us if the damage is spreading.

Signs an AC unit is leaking

Look for a drip from the air handler, a damp spot on a nearby ceiling, a puddle in a closet, musty air from the vents and a drain line that is dry when it should be dripping. A wall-mounted unit can leave streaks down the wall below it. Stains that look orange or pink may be rust from the pan or fittings.

Because the water is slow, it can go unnoticed for weeks. This is one reason AC leaks are not usually treated as emergencies, although they become one if the water reaches a light fixture or the ceiling starts to sag.

How the drying and repair work goes

The cause is repaired by an HVAC technician. The crew starts after that, mapping moisture with a meter and thermal camera, removing stained or soft drywall and wet insulation and drying the cavity with air movers. Because attic units can drip for a long time, insulation is often saturated well beyond the stain.

If the leak soaked a ceiling, our ceiling water damage repair page explains how those repairs are done. If the unit sits above a finished room, expect the work to include a stain-blocking primer so the mark does not return.

AC leaks in Johnstown homes

Johnstown summers can be humid, and units that run hard in July and August produce a lot of condensate. A condensate line that was fine in June can clog by late summer. Have a technician flush the drain line each spring, and add a float switch that shuts the unit off if the pan fills.

Window and wall units leak differently, since water exits at the back or bottom of the unit. If you rent, tell the landlord promptly and photograph the damage. For homes in Sidman and other outlying areas, the same maintenance applies.

Keeping the condensate line clear

The drain line carries condensate away from the air handler, and it can clog with algae and dust. A yearly flush with a cup of white vinegar poured into the access port helps. Many systems also have a float switch that shuts the unit off when the pan fills, which is worth adding if yours does not.

If you find water around the unit, turn the system off at the thermostat first so it stops making more.

Safety checklist and standards

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.

  • Close the supply valve to the appliance or turn the system off at the thermostat.
  • Unplug the unit only if the floor and your hands are dry.
  • Keep children and pets out of the wet room.
  • Do not run wet-dry equipment on wet ground without proper outlets.
