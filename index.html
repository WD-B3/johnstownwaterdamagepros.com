Water damage restoration in Johnstown

What water damage restoration actually involves

Water damage restoration is the work of stopping the damage after a leak, flood or storm. It means removing water, drying wet structure and contents, cleaning or removing what cannot be saved and rebuilding to the condition before the loss. In Johnstown, PA the 24/7 emergency line sends a crew to start that work as soon as it can, and the first call decides how much of the house is saved.

The reason speed matters is simple. Drywall, subfloor and insulation start to swell and stain within hours of getting wet, and a house that stays damp for several days can grow mold. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference the trade uses for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. This site does not claim any certification.

How a water damage job moves from call to rebuild

1

Call and describe the damage

Tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from, how far it has spread and whether the source is stopped. If it is safe, shut off the main water valve first.

2

Assess and document

A technician checks moisture levels with meters and a thermal camera, photographs the damage and writes down a scope of work before equipment goes in.

3

Extract and dry

Extraction units pull standing water, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure. Readings are logged each visit until the framing reaches its dry standard.

4

Repair and rebuild

Once everything is dry, damaged drywall, flooring and trim are replaced. The rebuild scope is explained before it starts.

Every step is written down. If your insurer asks what was wet, how wet it was and what was done about it, the readings and photos answer that. For a closer look at the drying phase, read about structural drying and dehumidification.

Insurance, in general terms

Sudden and accidental water damage, like a pipe that bursts overnight, is often covered by a homeowners policy. Slow leaks and neglected maintenance often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded and needs a separate flood policy. Read your policy or ask your insurer, because wording varies.

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises taking photos or video before cleaning up, keeping every receipt and not making permanent repairs until your insurer approves them. Writing down the name, title and date of each person you speak with at the insurance company also helps.

Local knowledge

Why Johnstown houses get wet, and what it means for you

Johnstown sits where the Stonycreek and Little Conemaugh rivers join to form the Conemaugh, which is why the city's flood history is so well known.

On May 31, 1889, the South Fork Dam failed and 2,209 people died, with debris piling against the Stone Bridge. The lesson for a homeowner today is not history trivia. It is that low ground near the rivers has flooded before, so a basement in Cambria City, Kernville or Woodvale deserves a hard look after any heavy rain.

After the March 1936 flood, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers built the Johnstown channel improvements between 1938 and 1943, covering 9.1 miles of the Conemaugh, Stonycreek and Little Conemaugh. In July 1977, about 12 inches of rain fell in 24 hours, six dams failed and the flow topped what the channels were designed for.

Channels reduce risk and do not remove it, so check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage before you need it.

Day to day, the water problems are smaller. Older homes in Downtown, Minersville, Moxham and Morrellville often have aging supply lines and tired water heaters, and Johnstown's hillside lots send runoff toward foundations. If your sewer line backs up, the Greater Johnstown Water Authority manages the city sanitary sewer system and can be reached at 814-533-4300.
