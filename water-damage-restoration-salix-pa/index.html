Water damage in Salix follows the seasons

Salix is a census-designated place in Adams Township, Cambria County, about 11 miles east of Johnstown, with ZIP code 15952. Water problems here change with the calendar, and the 24/7 emergency line is open in every season. Winter brings frozen pipes, spring thaws bring basement seepage, summer storms bring roof and window leaks and fall brings clogged gutters that spill against the foundation.

Use the sections below to match your season. If water is spreading now, call.

A year of water problems in Salix

Winter

Supply lines in exterior walls and unheated spaces freeze and split. See frozen pipe water damage.

Spring

Snowmelt and rain saturate the ground and push water through foundation walls and sump pits.

Summer

Heavy storms test roofs and flashing. See storm water damage.

Fall

Leaves clog gutters and downspouts, and water overflows toward the house.

When a Salix leak is an emergency

Any water that is still flowing is an emergency, and so is water that has reached a ceiling, an outlet or an appliance. After-hours calls are normal in Salix, and emergency dispatch is available around the clock with crews dispatched as availability allows. Close the main valve first and call from a dry spot.

Do not wait to see whether a stain stops growing. If a ceiling is bulging, keep clear and call.

About Salix

Salix is unincorporated, which means no borough council handles permits or ordinances. Earlier censuses listed it as Salix-Beauty Line Park, and the population was 1,043 in 2020, down from 1,259 in 2000. St. Michael, ZIP 15951, sits next to it and shares many of the same roads.

For rebuild permits, ask Adams Township and Cambria County. The Laurel Municipal Inspection Agency serves the City of Johnstown at (814) 471-0424, but it may not be the right office for Salix, so confirm before you start.

What the crew does in your Salix home

First the crew stops the spread, with extraction if needed, then maps moisture with a meter and thermal camera. Wet insulation and soft drywall are removed, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure and a written scope is shared with you before the repair starts. Read about the drying process on the structural drying page.

Salix is close to Elton and Sidman, so calls from all three are handled by the same dispatch line.

Making the call count

When you call, be ready to say where the water is coming from, whether it has stopped, which rooms are wet and whether anyone has turned off the main valve. The more clearly you describe the situation, the better the dispatcher can plan.

Have your address, a good phone number and your insurance company name on hand. If you rent, mention it so the landlord can be contacted.

Before you move anything

Take photos and a short video of the damage before cleanup begins. Then move items out of the wet area, starting with electronics, papers and photos. Lift furniture legs onto small blocks or foil so they do not stain the floor.

Leave wet drywall, carpet and insulation for the crew. Handling them yourself can make damage worse and can leave you without good documentation.

Drying is the long part

Water removal can take hours, but drying takes days. Expect equipment to run for several days and expect the noise. Readings will be taken until the structure reaches its goal. Leave equipment in place even if the room feels dry.

See structural drying and dehumidification for how the process works from start to finish.

A sudden pipe or storm failure is often covered, while gradual leaks usually are not. Read your policy or ask your insurer.

Safety checklist and standards

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.

  • Stay out of moving or deep water, and keep away from downed lines.
  • Do not enter a basement if water is near the panel, outlets or a furnace.
  • Call the utility if you smell gas or see sparks.
  • Wear boots and gloves for any contact with floodwater.
