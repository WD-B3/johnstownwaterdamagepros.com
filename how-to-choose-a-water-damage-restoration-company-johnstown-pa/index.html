How to Choose a Water Damage Restoration Company in Johnstown, PA

Seven questions to ask before you hire, and the answers that should make you walk away.

☎ (814) 358-8169

By the Johnstown Water Damage Pros team

The decision happens in a hurry

Most people choose a water damage company while water is still on the floor, which is the worst time to compare anything. A short list of questions helps you decide in a few minutes. This guide is for Johnstown, PA homeowners who want a company that will actually dry the house, document the work and explain the price.

If you are reading this with water spreading, stop the source, call 911 or the utility for any electrical concern and then use the questions below on the phone.

Question 1: do they answer at 2 a.m.?

Water damage does not keep office hours. A company that sends you to voicemail overnight gives the water a head start. Ask whether the line is staffed around the clock and what same-day response means in practice.

Be careful with promises of a fixed arrival time. Johnstown hills, snow and call volume change what is possible, and a precise promise made over the phone is often a sales line. A better answer is an honest explanation of how crews are dispatched.

Question 2: what standard do they follow?

The trade reference for water damage is ANSI/IICRC S500. Ask whether the crews work to it and how they set a drying goal. A real answer mentions moisture readings, a dry reference level and daily logs. A vague answer about big fans is a warning.

Do not accept claims you cannot check. If a company says it holds a certification, you can ask for the details and confirm them with the certifying body.

Question 3: will they explain the price first?

A fair company can explain what drives the cost before it starts, even when it cannot give an exact number from the phone. The drivers are the water category, the square footage, the materials involved, the days of drying and the rebuild scope. Our water extraction page shows how those pieces fit together.

Be skeptical of a quote that arrives with no site visit, and of anyone who says insurance will pay for everything. No honest company can promise a claim outcome.

Question 4: how do they handle your insurer?

You pay for the policy, and the insurer decides coverage. A restoration company can document the loss clearly, which helps the claim, but it should never tell you what your policy covers. Ask for the moisture log, the photos and the written scope in a form your adjuster can use.

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises taking photos or video before cleanup, keeping receipts and not making permanent repairs until the insurer approves. A good company works with that advice rather than against it.

Question 5: what is not included?

The most useful answer is a list of limits. A company that does extraction and drying but not plumbing repairs should say so. One that does not do mold remediation should say so too. Honest limits are a good sign.

For a flood or sewage loss, ask how they handle contaminated materials. Our sewage backup cleanup page explains what is removed and why, so you can judge the answer you hear.

Checking what a company should tell you

In Johnstown, permits for repairs and renovations go through the Laurel Municipal Inspection Agency, which handles building permit services for the city, and the agency can be reached at (814) 471-0424. A good company will tell you early whether your repair scope needs one, since painting and flooring are exempt but most other repairs are not.

City Hall is at 401 Main Street and can be reached at (814) 539-2504. If you live outside the city, in places like Sidman or Elton, the township and county offices apply instead, and a company should say so rather than assume.

Warning signs worth walking away from

Walk away if a company demands a large cash deposit before it has assessed the damage, refuses to put the scope in writing, will not say whether it is insured or tells you to avoid calling your insurer. Treat a door-to-door storm chaser the same way, since out-of-area crews often leave before the work is finished.

Also watch for fabricated trust. Review badges, award seals and customer counts that cannot be checked are not proof of anything.

Standards and sources

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.
