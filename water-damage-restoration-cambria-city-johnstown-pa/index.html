Cambria City, across the Conemaugh River from the old ironworks

Cambria City lies on the opposite bank of the Conemaugh River from the Cambria Iron Works. Its National Register district covers about 39 acres, roughly bounded by Broad Street, Tenth Avenue and the Conemaugh River, and was listed in 1991. The listing calls it a predominantly working-class residential area with a small business district, churches, schools and a fire station.

The district has 198 contributing buildings. Most date from 1890 to 1920, and some were standing before the 1889 flood. The dwellings are described as two-story, balloon-frame, detached and semi-detached houses.

Shared walls, rentals and open wall cavities

Two facts from the neighborhood record shape a water loss here. Heritage Johnstown notes that many Cambria City homes were built as rental properties, and the district listing includes semi-detached houses. In a semi-detached house a leak on the other side can reach the wall you share, so tell the crew which wall that is. If you rent a unit, report the problem to your landlord in writing and send photos.

Balloon framing, as the listing describes, uses long studs that run past the floor levels. That can leave wall cavities open between stories, so water that enters one cavity can travel up or down. A good moisture survey checks the floor above and below a stain instead of stopping at the paint.

Before the crew arrives

Keep it simple and stay safe.

Water supply

Close the valve for the fixture or the main if it is dry and easy to reach.

Electricity

Stay out of standing water that touches outlets or cords and call 911 or the utility for any electrical danger.

Sewage

If water came up a drain, keep people and pets away and do not clean it up with household fans or a shop vacuum.

The river, the flood record and what we do not claim

Cambria City sits near the Conemaugh River, and Johnstown floods in 1889, 1936 and 1977 are well documented. After the 1936 flood the Army Corps of Engineers built paved channels through the city between 1938 and 1943. We have not verified which Cambria City addresses were affected in any of those events, and neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified.

Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer whether flood damage is covered, since flood is often handled separately from other water damage. Many calls to a restoration company have nothing to do with a river at all.

Drying a house that shares its walls

After extraction, the crew sets air movers and dehumidifiers and takes readings each visit. In a shared-wall house, the plan should say how the shared wall is dried and whether the neighbor must be told. The water extraction and structural drying pages explain those stages.

Cambria City and the Minersville district are listed as neighboring industrial communities, and the Minersville Bridge is listed as part of the Cambria City district.

Landlords, tenants and insurance

The lease and the building policy decide who handles repairs, and a tenant should also check a renters policy for belongings. Photos and moisture readings can support the record. Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and the policy, and we do not decide coverage.

Safety checklist and standards

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.

  • Stay out of moving or deep water, and keep away from downed lines.
  • Do not enter a basement if water is near the panel, outlets or a furnace.
  • Call the utility if you smell gas or see sparks.
  • Wear boots and gloves for any contact with floodwater.
