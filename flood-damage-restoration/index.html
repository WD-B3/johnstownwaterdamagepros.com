Flood damage in a river city

Flood damage restoration is the cleanup, drying and repair that follow rising water from a river, creek or saturated ground. Johnstown has a long record of it, and the 24/7 emergency line takes flood calls in every Johnstown ZIP code. If water is still coming in, get people and pets to higher ground, leave the electricity alone unless you can reach the panel dry, and call us from a safe spot.

Floodwater is not like a pipe leak. It carries silt, sewage and runoff, so do not wade through it or touch wet items without gloves and boots.

When floodwater is an emergency

Floodwater inside a home is always urgent, because it is treated as Category 3 water and the contamination reaches every porous surface it touches. Night and weekend flooding is common in Cambria County because storms stall over the valleys, so the line is open around the clock and same-day response is offered based on crew availability.

Do not turn on a furnace, water heater or any appliance that sat in floodwater. Do not run power to wet rooms. If the water rose past outlets, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Johnstown has been here before

On May 31, 1889, the South Fork Dam failed and 2,209 people died, with a wall of debris stopping at the Stone Bridge. After the March 1936 flood, the Army Corps of Engineers built concrete-lined channels through the city between 1938 and 1943. In July 1977, nearly 12 inches of rain fell in 24 hours, six dams failed and the flow reached 115,000 cubic feet per second against a design limit of 81,500.

The practical lesson is that the channels lower the risk and do not remove it. Neighborhoods like Woodvale, Cambria City, Kernville and Old Conemaugh Borough sit close to the water. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer whether your policy covers flooding.

How flood restoration works

The crew starts with safety, then extraction. Water extraction removes the bulk water, and then ruined carpet, pad, insulation and low drywall come out. Surfaces that stay are cleaned and disinfected, and the structure is dried with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers until moisture meter readings meet the drying goal.

Flooded basements are the most common case. Our basement water damage cleanup page covers the steps for the lowest level specifically, including sump pump checks and finished-space contents.

What you can keep and what goes

Hard, non-porous items like glass, metal and some plastics can be cleaned and sanitized. Upholstery, mattresses, particleboard furniture and most paper goods that sat in floodwater are not worth keeping. Solid wood furniture is a judgment call, and photos before disposal help your insurer.

Keep a list of what you throw away, with photos. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises documenting damage before cleaning up and keeping receipts for emergency repairs.

What shapes flood cleanup costs

Price follows the height of the water, how long it stood, the square footage affected, how much flooring and drywall has to come out and how many days of drying are needed. We explain scope and price before work starts. Want your real number? Call (814) 358-8169.

Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies. A separate flood policy is what typically responds, so read yours or ask your insurer.

Safety checklist and standards

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.

  • Stay out of moving or deep water, and keep away from downed lines.
  • Do not enter a basement if water is near the panel, outlets or a furnace.
  • Call the utility if you smell gas or see sparks.
  • Wear boots and gloves for any contact with floodwater.
