Seward sits across a county line

Seward is a borough in Westmoreland County, incorporated on January 23, 1904 and governed by a borough council. ZIP databases list 15954 under Indiana County, even though the borough is in Westmoreland County. The 1977 flood and Seward

Seward is listed among the communities hit by the July 1977 flood, when nearly 12 inches of rain fell in 24 hours and six dams failed. Low ground anywhere can collect water when storms stall, so check your basement after heavy rain.

That history is a reason to look at your own risk. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, ask your insurer whether flood is covered and keep a plan for where to move belongings. For a closer look at how flood work goes, read flood damage cleanup.

How restoration proceeds

The crew extracts water, maps moisture with meters and a thermal camera, removes wet materials and dries the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers. A written scope comes first, and the price is explained before work starts. If water is coming from a basement, see basement water damage cleanup.

Seward is close to Armagh and to Johnstown neighborhoods on the west side, and the nearest town with its own page is Nanty Glo. We never promise an arrival time, since road and weather conditions vary, but same-day response is offered based on crew availability.

What to have ready

Write down the address, which rooms are wet and when you first noticed the problem. Note whether the water is clear or discolored, and whether the source has been stopped. These details help the dispatcher decide what equipment to send.

If you are not at home, tell the dispatcher who will let the crew in. A key arrangement avoids delays.

Protect belongings while you wait

Move small valuables, rugs and electronics to dry rooms. Place foil or small wooden blocks under furniture legs. Open closet doors if the closet is wet so air can circulate. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.

Take photos before and after moving things, and keep a list of anything damaged.

Talking with your insurer

Call your insurer early, describe what happened and ask what they need from you. Keep a notebook of calls with dates, names and what was said. Ask about deductibles and whether the policy covers the type of water involved.

The restoration company can supply photos, readings and a written scope. The insurer decides coverage, and no company can promise an outcome.

Safety checklist and standards

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.

  • Stay out of moving or deep water, and keep away from downed lines.
  • Do not enter a basement if water is near the panel, outlets or a furnace.
  • Call the utility if you smell gas or see sparks.
  • Wear boots and gloves for any contact with floodwater.
