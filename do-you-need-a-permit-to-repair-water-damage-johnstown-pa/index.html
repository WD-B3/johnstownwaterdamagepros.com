Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Johnstown, PA?

What the city requires, who to call and the questions to settle before the rebuild starts.

☎ (814) 358-8169

By the Johnstown Water Damage Pros team

The short answer

In the City of Johnstown, the city says building and demolition permits are required for all demolition, repairs, renovations, remodels and new construction. Painting and flooring are exempt. That means repairing water damage often involves a permit if it goes beyond painting and flooring, such as replacing drywall, repairing framing or reworking plumbing and electrical.

The rules can differ for other municipalities. If you live outside the city, ask your borough or township.

Emergency work and rebuild work

Emergency mitigation, such as extraction and drying, is usually not what people think of as a permitted repair. The rebuild is where permits come up. Ask the inspection agency whether your specific scope needs one, because the answer depends on what you replace.

A good restoration company tells you early whether it expects permits and who will pull them. See wall and drywall water damage repair for what a rebuild may include.

Who pulls the permit

The city says permits can be issued to the owner, an authorized agent or the contractor doing the work. Ask your contractor which one it will be, and who pays the fee. If the contractor expects you to pull it, find out what you must sign.

Do not start work and ask later. Unpermitted work can cause trouble with your insurer, a future sale or an inspection.

What the inspector may look at

Inspections vary with the scope. For plumbing, electrical and structural repairs, an inspector may check the work before it is covered up. Plan for that in your schedule, since closing a wall before an inspection can mean opening it again.

Keep copies of permits and approvals. They are part of the repair record that a future buyer or insurer may ask to see.

Which repairs usually trigger a permit question

The line between a cosmetic fix and a permitted repair is easy to cross without noticing. Replacing a few sheets of drywall is one thing. Rebuilding a wall, moving plumbing, replacing a panel or changing wiring is another. The more structural, electrical or plumbing work involved, the more likely the inspection agency will want to review it.

If your water loss is large enough that the rebuild touches framing, wiring or supply lines, assume you should ask before ordering materials.

How to ask the right way

1

Describe the work

Write down which rooms are affected and what will be replaced, such as drywall, insulation, subfloor or fixtures.

2

Call the agency

Phone the inspection agency and ask whether your list needs a permit and what documents they want.

3

Write down the answer

Note the date, the name of the person and what they said, and keep it with your repair records.

4

Tell the contractor

Share the answer so the contractor can plan inspections into the schedule.

Why the paperwork matters later

A permit record shows a future buyer, an appraiser or an insurer that the repair was reviewed. Without it, a good repair can still raise questions at resale. If a past owner did unpermitted work, a home inspector may flag it and a lender may ask about it.

Keep permits, inspection sign-offs, invoices and drying logs together in one folder. It takes ten minutes and can save weeks of back and forth years from now.

Plan the schedule around inspections

If an inspection is needed, the work has to stay open until the inspector has seen it. That can mean a pause between rough-in and closing the wall. Ask the contractor how the schedule handles that wait so you are not surprised by a gap.

Drying equipment should stay in place for the whole drying period, so ask how the permit steps fit around it.

Permits for Johnstown rebuilds

The City of Johnstown is a member of the Laurel Municipal Inspection Agency, which provides its building permit services. The agency can be reached at (814) 471-0424 or info@LMIAgency.org. City Hall is at 401 Main Street, and the number is (814) 539-2504.

Commercial and some residential projects need construction drawings stamped by a Pennsylvania registered architect or engineer. If your water damage affects a business, see commercial water damage restoration for how larger losses are handled, and ask the agency about stamped plans.

For Salix and Elton, ask the township.

Outside the city

Boroughs and townships set their own rules. A borough like Nanty Glo or Seward has its own office, and an unincorporated place like Salix or Elton falls under the township and the county. Do not assume the City of Johnstown rules apply.

When in doubt, call the office and ask in plain words: I have water damage and plan to replace drywall and flooring, do I need a permit?

Standards and sources

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.
