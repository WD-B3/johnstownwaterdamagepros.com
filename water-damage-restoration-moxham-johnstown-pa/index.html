For homes in the Moxham Historic District and the streets around it in southern Johnstown, from a failed supply line to rain that finds its way indoors.

☎ (814) 358-8169

Moxham, a planned residential grid in southern Johnstown

Moxham sits in the southern part of Johnstown. It was laid out in the late 1880s as a community of privately owned residential lots rather than a company-owned town. Planning began in 1887 under Tom Johnson and his brother Albert, a survey followed in 1888 and the plan was revised several times after the 1889 flood.

The National Register district covers about 70 acres and lists 330 contributing buildings, 315 of them dwellings. Most date from 1890 to 1930, and the listed styles include Bungalow and Craftsman, Colonial Revival and American Foursquare. That mix of sizes matters when water gets in, because a leak in a compact bungalow and a leak in a large foursquare call for different drying plans.

What the flood record does and does not tell you

The National Register summary describes Moxham as an area relatively spared by flooding, and much of its growth came after 1889. That describes where the neighborhood lies. It is not a promise about any one house, and neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here, so check the FEMA flood map for your address.

In a home like yours, water damage more often starts inside. A washing machine hose, a water heater fitting, a supply line behind a wall or a roof leak can soak a room just as thoroughly as a river can, and none of those depend on a flood map.

If water is spreading in your Moxham home

Four steps, in this order, while you wait for help.

1

Stop the source

Close the shutoff valve for the fixture or the main valve, but only if you can reach it without standing in water.

2

Respect electricity

Do not touch outlets, panels or cords in wet areas. If water is near electrical equipment, call 911 or the utility.

3

Protect what is dry

Lift small items, books and electronics off wet floors and keep children and pets out of the room.

4

Photograph and call

Take photos from a safe spot, then call (814) 358-8169 and describe what you see.

Blocks that climb away from Central Avenue

The original plan placed the town on the east side of Central Avenue and carried it up the gradually sloping land into the surrounding hills. Homes on upper and lower blocks therefore sit at different heights. After heavy rain, look at the lowest level of your house first, and check that downspouts empty away from the foundation. This is a general pattern for sloped lots and not a record of past problems on any Moxham street.

If your house has a basement or crawl space, see basement water damage cleanup or crawl space water damage cleanup. We do not assume every home has one.

How the work goes in a finished house

The first job is removing standing water, which is explained on the water extraction page. Next the crew measures moisture in floors, walls and ceilings with meters and a thermal camera, so the wet areas are mapped rather than guessed at. Air movers and dehumidifiers then run until readings reach the drying goal. The full process is on the structural drying page.

Wood floors, plaster and trim dry at different speeds, and some materials cannot be saved. Ask the crew to show you readings and to explain which materials they plan to keep and which they plan to remove.

Cost and insurance

Price depends on the amount of water, its category, the rooms and materials affected and the number of drying days. Photos and moisture readings can help document damage for your records. Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and your policy, so ask your insurer. We do not decide coverage.

Safety checklist and standards

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.

  • Stay out of moving or deep water, and keep away from downed lines.
  • Do not enter a basement if water is near the panel, outlets or a furnace.
  • Call the utility if you smell gas or see sparks.
  • Wear boots and gloves for any contact with floodwater.
