Basement water: where it comes from and what to do

A basement collects water from above and from the ground around it. Basement water damage cleanup is the removal of that water, the drying of the structure and the repair of finishes and contents. In Johnstown, PA the 24/7 line takes basement calls every day of the year. Stay out of the water if the electrical panel or outlets are involved, and if the basement is rising, do not go down the stairs.

Shut off the water supply if it is a pipe leak, and call us from upstairs.

When basement water is an emergency

Water in a basement that holds a furnace, water heater and panel is a safety hazard as well as a damage issue, and it often happens at night during storms. The line stays open after hours, with same-day response when crews are available. A basement that fills quickly may be a sign of a larger problem, like a flooded creek or a sewer backup.

Electricity and water do not mix. If water is touching outlets, appliances or the panel, call 911 or the utility before calling us.

Basements in a river valley

Johnstown's basements have a history. The 1889 flood and the 1977 flood both put water into homes across the city. Neighborhoods near the rivers, like Cambria City, Kernville, Woodvale and Old Conemaugh Borough, sit close to the water table, and Johnstown's hillside lots send runoff toward foundations.

After heavy rain, a wet basement is common even without a flood. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, test your sump pump and make sure the discharge line runs away from the house. Ask your insurer about water backup coverage as well.

How the cleanup works

The crew extracts the water, then moves out wet contents and removes carpet, pad and soaked drywall. On a finished basement, they check studs and insulation behind the walls. Concrete holds moisture, so drying is slower, and dehumidifiers run for several days.

Photos are taken as each layer comes out, and readings are logged. Items on shelves and in boxes can be sorted, with non-porous items cleaned and porous ones discarded. See structural drying for how readings guide the finish.

What restoration is not

Restoration is not waterproofing. Fixing a leaky foundation, regrading or installing drains is a separate trade, and a waterproofing contractor can evaluate it after the basement is dry. Be wary of anyone who promises to make a basement never leak again.

The City of Johnstown requires building permits for repairs and renovations, with painting and flooring exempt. Ask the Laurel Municipal Inspection Agency at (814) 471-0424 about your rebuild. In Nanty Glo and other towns, the borough or township office handles that question.

What affects the cost

Safety checklist and standards

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.

  • Stay out of moving or deep water, and keep away from downed lines.
  • Do not enter a basement if water is near the panel, outlets or a furnace.
  • Call the utility if you smell gas or see sparks.
  • Wear boots and gloves for any contact with floodwater.
