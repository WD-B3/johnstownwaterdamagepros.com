Minersville, a small hillside district north of the Conemaugh River

Minersville is a compact working-class neighborhood of privately and company-built housing on the north side of the Conemaugh River. It was tied mainly to the former Cambria Iron Lower Works. The National Register district, listed in 1995, covers 13.4 acres roughly along Connelly Avenue, Honan Avenue, Garvey Place and Iron Street.

The listing counts 92 residences and one fraternal club. Construction ran from 1880 to 1945, with the strongest growth in the early 1900s. The listed area spans Johnstown and West Taylor Township, so if you are unsure which municipality your address falls in, ask before you apply for a repair permit.

Why the hillside matters for water

The listing credits the hillside location with helping Minersville survive the 1889 flood. That is history, and it does not mean a basement stays dry. On a slope, rain runs downhill and can press against the uphill wall of a house or collect at the lowest level.

We have not verified a Minersville drainage record, so treat this as a general pattern for sloped lots. After a hard rain, check that downspouts discharge away from the house, that window wells are clear and that any sump pump runs. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified, so look up your address on the FEMA flood map.

Safe first steps on a sloped lot

Watch your footing as well as the water.

1

Stay out of deep or moving water

Do not walk into a flooded lower level if water is near outlets, cords or the furnace.

2

Close the valve if you can

Shut the main or the fixture valve only if you can do it from dry ground.

3

Watch the steps

Wet stairs and sloped paths are slippery. Keep a light on and take your time.

4

What the crew does once it arrives

Standing water comes out first, using pumps and extractors as described on the water extraction page. The crew then checks floors, walls and ceilings for hidden moisture and sets drying equipment. Readings are taken at each visit until the structure reaches its drying goal.

Smaller houses have fewer rooms, but the walls and floors still need to reach their targets before anything is closed up. Ask for the readings, and ask which materials the crew wants to replace.

Cost and insurance

Price depends on the amount of water, its category, the materials affected and the drying days. Photos and moisture readings can support your records. Coverage depends on the cause and your policy, so contact your insurer. We do not decide coverage.

Safety checklist and standards

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.

  • Stay out of moving or deep water, and keep away from downed lines.
  • Do not enter a basement if water is near the panel, outlets or a furnace.
  • Call the utility if you smell gas or see sparks.
  • Wear boots and gloves for any contact with floodwater.
