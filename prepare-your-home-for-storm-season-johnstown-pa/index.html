How to Prepare Your Home for Storm Season in Johnstown, PA

A short checklist to run through before the next heavy rain, from the roof down to the sump pit.

☎ (814) 358-8169

By the Johnstown Water Damage Pros team

Prepare before the sky turns dark

Storm damage is easier to prevent than to repair, and most of the prevention is simple. This checklist helps Johnstown homeowners reduce the chance of water getting in when heavy rain arrives. Most items take minutes, and a few are worth doing once a year.

Do not wait for a warning. Storms that stall over the Johnstown valley have caused major damage before, and preparation helps even in a smaller storm.

Work from the top down.

Roof, gutters and the outside

Walk around the house after any storm and look up. Missing shingles, bent flashing and sagging gutters are early warnings. A roofer can fix them cheaply compared with the cost of an interior leak. If a leak does reach the ceiling, see ceiling water damage repair for how it is handled.

Clean gutters at least twice a year, and again after leaves fall. A clogged gutter spills water against the house at exactly the point where it can do the most harm.

The basement and sump pump

Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and watch that the pump starts and the water is moved out. Check that the discharge line is clear and does not just drain back toward the house. A battery backup keeps the pump running during a power outage, which is common in storms.

Move valuables off the basement floor and onto shelves. If your basement floods, the steps are on the basement water damage cleanup page.

Insurance and documents

Review your policy now. Ask whether it covers wind and rain, flood, sump pump failure and sewer backup. Many homeowners find gaps only after a loss. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department encourages policyholders to review coverage ahead of storms.

Take photos of each room and your belongings and store them somewhere safe, such as a cloud account. Keep a list of your policy number and the insurer's phone number where you can reach it from your phone.

Inside the house

Test the sump pump by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching it turn on and drain. A battery backup is worth considering because storms often knock out power. Keep valuables off the basement floor and store papers in sealed plastic bins.

Know where your main shutoff and electrical panel are. If water rises near outlets, leave the basement and call the utility before anyone goes back in.

Outside the house

Clean gutters and make sure downspouts end several feet from the foundation. Check that the soil next to the house slopes away. Trim branches that hang over the roof and look for loose shingles or damaged flashing.

Window wells should be clear, and covers help keep debris and water out. Move grills, furniture and other loose items that could damage windows in high wind.

Write down what you own

A room by room video of your belongings is one of the most useful things you can do before storm season. Narrate what each item is and open drawers and closets. Store the file somewhere other than the house, such as cloud storage.

Keep your insurance policy number and agent contact in your phone so you can reach them quickly.

After the storm passes

When the storm is over, walk the property before going into the basement. Look for downed lines, standing water and cracked foundation walls. If anything looks unsafe, call the utility or emergency services.

Photograph all damage before you start cleanup and call your insurer promptly. Then call the restoration line for any water that got inside.

Johnstown storms and floods

The July 1977 flood began on the night of July 19, when nearly 12 inches of rain fell in 24 hours and six dams failed. The March 1936 flood led the Army Corps of Engineers to build channel improvements between 1938 and 1943. These events are the best reminder that a stalled storm can overwhelm a valley.

The National Weather Service issues forecasts and warnings for the area, and the county emergency management agency can tell you about local alerts. If you live in a nearby borough such as Nanty Glo, where a creek runs through town, check alerts for your own area. For the aftermath of heavy wind and rain, see storm water damage.

Know your shutoffs and your plan

Everyone in the house should know where the main water valve is. Keep a flashlight and a basic kit where you can reach it. Decide where you would go if water rose and how you would get out.

If a storm does cause damage, stay safe first. Avoid downed lines and standing water near outlets, and call us when it is safe.

Standards and sources

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.
