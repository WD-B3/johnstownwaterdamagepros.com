Old Conemaugh Borough, a Victorian-era district inside Johnstown

Old Conemaugh Borough is a historic district within the city of Johnstown. The National Register listing, dated 1995, covers about 33 acres roughly bounded by Railroad Street, Adams Street, Steel Street and Church Avenue. It lists 330 contributing buildings in a predominantly working-class residential area.

The listed architecture is mostly Queen Anne and Second Empire, with a few high-style Victorian dwellings. Individual buildings range from an 1832 inn to a house built in 1904, along with churches and schools. The name points back to the borough that Conemaugh once was, and the address on your mail will say Johnstown.

Original woodwork, plaster and what to say on the first call

If your house still has original trim, built-in cabinets, stair parts or plaster walls, say so when you call. Whether those finishes can be dried in place depends on the material and on how long it stayed wet, and the crew should explain the reasoning instead of simply cutting it out.

Keep a sample of any damaged trim, and photograph it before it is removed. Matching profiles later is much easier with a piece in hand. Some materials cannot be saved, and an honest crew will tell you which ones.

What the listing says about flooding

The National Register summary for this district does not discuss flooding, and neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here. Johnstown floods in 1889, 1936 and 1977 are well documented, but we have not confirmed which streets of this district were affected, so we make no claim about any address.

Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask your insurer whether flood damage is covered separately from other water damage.

Drying a house with detailed interiors

After extraction, the crew measures moisture in floors, walls and ceilings and sets air movers and dehumidifiers. Detailed interiors may need slower, more careful drying than plain drywall rooms. The water extraction and structural drying pages describe each stage, and the crew should give you readings at each visit.

Ask when you will see the next set of readings and what number the crew is aiming for before equipment is removed.

Cost and insurance

Price depends on the amount of water, its category, the materials and the drying days. Photos, receipts and moisture readings can support your records. Coverage depends on the cause and your policy, so ask your insurer. We do not decide coverage.

Safety checklist and standards

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.

  • Stay out of moving or deep water, and keep away from downed lines.
  • Do not enter a basement if water is near the panel, outlets or a furnace.
  • Call the utility if you smell gas or see sparks.
  • Wear boots and gloves for any contact with floodwater.
