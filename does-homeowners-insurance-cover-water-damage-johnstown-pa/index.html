Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage? A Johnstown, PA Guide

Sudden versus gradual, inside versus outside, and the exclusions that surprise people.

☎ (814) 358-8169

By the Johnstown Water Damage Pros team

The short answer is sometimes

Whether homeowners insurance covers water damage depends on the cause. In general, sudden and accidental damage, like a pipe that bursts or a water heater that fails, is often covered. Slow leaks that were not fixed, neglected maintenance and flood damage often are not. Policies differ, so read yours or ask your insurer.

We are a restoration line, not an insurer or an adviser, so we cannot say what your policy pays. What we can do is explain the common patterns so your questions are better.

Flood is a separate policy

Standard homeowners policies generally exclude flood. If you live near the Stonycreek, Little Conemaugh or Conemaugh rivers, or in a low spot, a flood policy is something to ask about before you need it. These policies often have a waiting period, so waiting for the storm is too late.

Our flood damage cleanup page explains what cleanup involves after a flood, and how it differs from a plumbing loss.

How to document a claim

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises taking photos or video before cleaning up, keeping every receipt for money spent on the damage and not making permanent repairs until your insurer approves them. Make only the immediate repairs needed to prevent more damage. Write down the name, title and date of each person you speak with at the insurance company.

A restoration company can add photos, moisture readings and a written scope. Those records show what was wet and how wet it was, and what was done about it.

Deductibles and what the check does not cover

Your deductible is what you pay before coverage begins, and some policies set special deductibles for certain events. Coverage may also exclude the cost of repairing the pipe itself while covering the water damage that followed. Ask what is included.

Depreciation matters for flooring and carpet. Ask whether your policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value. These are the questions that decide how much you receive.

Read the words sudden and accidental

Many homeowners policies are written around sudden and accidental events, such as a pipe that bursts overnight. Gradual problems, like a slow leak over months or seepage from a lack of maintenance, are often treated differently. The wording varies by policy, so the only reliable answer is in your own document or from your agent.

Do not guess. Ask your agent directly whether your situation counts as sudden, and ask for the answer in writing if you can.

Keep records from the first hour

Take photos and video before anything is moved, keep receipts for emergency costs and write down the date and time you noticed the problem. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, unless they are unsafe. These steps are in line with the advice the Pennsylvania Insurance Department gives consumers.

A restoration company can supply moisture readings and a written scope, which your adjuster may find useful. It cannot promise what the insurer will pay.

Insurance in a river city

Johnstown's flood history is why flood insurance is a local conversation. The 1889, 1936 and 1977 floods are the main events, and after 1936 the Army Corps of Engineers built channel improvements between 1938 and 1943. In 1977 the flow exceeded what the channels were designed to carry, so protection is not the same as immunity.

Ask your insurer whether your address is in a mapped flood zone, and check the FEMA flood map. If you live in a nearby town such as Seward, check the same map for your address, since flood risk varies by block.

Homeowners in Nanty Glo, where a creek runs through the borough, should ask the same question.

Should you file a small claim?

A small claim may not be worth it if the deductible is close to the cost, and claims can affect future premiums or renewals. But do not decide alone. Ask your agent how a claim would be handled and whether a call to ask a question counts as a claim.

Whatever you decide, dry the structure. Hidden moisture can turn a small loss into a large one, and drying records are useful if you later change your mind. See burst pipe water damage for how a typical covered loss is handled.

Standards and sources

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.
