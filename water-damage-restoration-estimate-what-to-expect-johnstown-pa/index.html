What a Water Damage Restoration Estimate Should Include in Johnstown, PA

The sections of a good estimate, the line items worth questioning and the difference between emergency work and rebuild.

☎ (814) 358-8169

By the Johnstown Water Damage Pros team

Why the estimate matters

A water damage estimate is the document you and your insurer will use to decide what is done and what it costs. A good one is specific. A bad one is a single number with no explanation. Knowing what should be in it helps you compare quotes and avoid paying for work you do not need.

No honest company can give an exact price without seeing the damage. What you can expect is a written scope that explains the work and a price tied to it.

Emergency work versus rebuild

Emergency services stop the damage. They include extraction, protective measures, removal of ruined materials and drying. The rebuild puts the structure back, with new drywall, flooring, paint and trim. A good estimate separates the two, since you may choose a different contractor for the second phase.

The drying phase is explained in detail on our structural drying page, including the equipment you should expect and the readings that decide when it is done.

Equipment and days: the common line items

Charges for equipment are often per unit per day. That is normal, and it is why the number of days matters. Ask how the crew will decide when to remove equipment, and how many readings will be taken. A claim of one flat price for any amount of drying should make you ask what is included.

Ask whether the equipment count matches the size of the wet area. Too little equipment drags out drying. Too much costs more with no benefit. A moisture map justifies the number.

Questions to ask about line items

If an item is vague, ask what it means. A line like demolition should say what is being demolished and why. A line like antimicrobial treatment should say where and for what reason. A line like contents should explain whether items are being cleaned or discarded.

Also ask what is not in the estimate. If hidden damage is found behind a wall, will the scope change, and how will you be told? A good company explains how changes are approved.

Why an estimate may change

Water damage hides behind walls and under floors. A first estimate is based on what can be seen and measured on day one. If the crew opens a wall and finds wet insulation or framing, the scope may grow. A good company tells you about the change before doing the extra work.

Ask how changes are handled and whether you will get a written update.

Pay attention to the order of work

Emergency work comes first, then drying, then cleaning and finally rebuild. Deposits for rebuild work should come after drying is done and the scope is clear. Be careful about large payments upfront for work that will not start for weeks.

If your insurer is paying, ask whether payment goes to you, to the company or both.

Estimates and permits in Johnstown

If your rebuild involves repairs or renovations in the City of Johnstown, ask whether a building permit is needed, since the city requires them for repairs and renovations, with painting and flooring exempt. The Laurel Municipal Inspection Agency issues them and can be reached at (814) 471-0424. A good estimate says who pulls the permit and who pays for it.

Outside the city, in towns like Elton, the permit office differs. Ask the township or county before you sign, and ask whether the estimate assumes one. See wall and drywall water damage repair for an example of how a rebuild scope reads.

A borough such as Seward has its own office to ask.

Red flags

Be careful with a quote given over the phone with no site visit, a demand for full payment up front, a scope with no moisture readings and a refusal to put anything in writing. Be careful too with pressure to sign on the spot, especially after a storm.

Your insurer may also have its own estimate. If the two differ, ask both parties to explain the difference line by line. It is often a matter of scope, not honesty.

Standards and sources

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.
