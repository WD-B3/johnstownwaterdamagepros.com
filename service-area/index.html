Johnstown ZIP codes we cover

Inside the city we cover ZIP codes 15901, 15902, 15904, 15905, 15906, 15907, 15909 and 15915. That includes Downtown, Cambria City, Minersville, Moxham, Kernville, Woodvale, Morrellville and Old Conemaugh Borough. If you are not sure whether your address is covered, call the 24/7 line and ask.

Nearby towns with their own pages

Sidman (15955), Salix (15952) and Elton (15934) sit east and southeast of the city in Cambria County. Nanty Glo (15943) lies to the northeast in the Blacklick Creek valley. Seward (15954) is a borough across the county line in Westmoreland County.

We also take calls from Armagh, South Fork, Mineral Point, Parkhill and Dunlo. They do not need separate pages, and the dispatcher can tell you what applies.

How coverage works

Coverage means the dispatch line takes your call and can send a crew to your address. It does not mean every town has an office. Calls are answered around the clock, and the dispatcher asks where you are, what the water is doing and whether the source has been stopped.

Same-day response is offered when available. We do not promise an arrival time, because Johnstown hills, snow, river valleys and call volume change what is possible on any given night.

Why the town matters

Johnstown sits in a river valley where the Stonycreek and Little Conemaugh meet. Towns to the east and north sit along other creeks and ridges. Water problems differ from one valley to the next, which is why the area pages explain local conditions such as creek flooding, older supply lines and basement seepage.

The dispatcher can tell you which page fits your address, and the page for your town lists the ZIP codes it covers.

What to have ready when you call

Short answers get a crew moving faster.

Your address

Street, town and ZIP code, plus a nearby cross street if the house is hard to find.

The source

Where the water is coming from and whether it has been stopped at the main valve.

The rooms

Which rooms or floors are wet and roughly how much water is standing.

Access

Who will meet the crew, or how they can get in if you are not home.

Services available in every area

The same nineteen services apply across the whole coverage area. Common calls include burst pipes, flooded basements, water heater leaks and storm damage. The services overview lists all of them by cause.

Drying follows the same standard everywhere, with readings taken until the structure reaches its goal. See structural drying for how that works.

If your town is not listed

Call and ask. Cambria County has many small communities, and the dispatcher can tell you whether your address can be served. If it cannot, we would rather say so quickly than keep you waiting while water spreads.

In any emergency involving electricity, gas or sewage, call 911 or the utility first.

Reading the ZIP codes

ZIP codes in the area do not always match town lines. A mailing address may say one town while the home sits in a neighboring township or borough, which matters for permits and utilities. If you are unsure, use the address on your tax or utility bill and tell the dispatcher.

Each area page lists the ZIP codes it covers so you can check quickly before calling.

Seasonal patterns across the area

Winter brings frozen and split pipes. Spring brings melt, rain and saturated ground that push water into basements. Summer storms test roofs and windows, and fall leaves clog gutters. The seasonal risks guide lays out what to check in each season.

Calls come in at every hour, and storms can fill the phones, so call early when you see trouble.

Utilities and local offices

For a sewer backup in the city, the Greater Johnstown Water Authority can be reached at 814-533-4300. For permits in the city, the Laurel Municipal Inspection Agency can be reached at (814) 471-0424. Outside the city, boroughs and townships have their own offices.

Our permit guide explains what to ask before a rebuild.

Emergencies that are not water damage

Some calls belong elsewhere first. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility. If water is touching outlets, panels or appliances, stay out and call the electric utility or 911. If someone is hurt, call 911 before anything else.

Once the situation is safe, the 24/7 line is ready to start extraction and drying.
