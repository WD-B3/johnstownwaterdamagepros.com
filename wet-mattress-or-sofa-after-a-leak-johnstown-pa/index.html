What to Do About a Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak in Johnstown, PA

Which furniture can be dried, which should go and how to handle both without making it worse.

☎ (814) 358-8169

By the Johnstown Water Damage Pros team

It depends on what kind of water it was

A wet mattress or sofa is a question people ask after the water is gone. The answer depends on the water category and how long the item stayed wet. Clean water from a supply line that was caught quickly is very different from sewage or floodwater, and different again from a roof leak that soaked a sofa overnight.

The rule is simple. Water from a clean source gives you a chance to save things. Water from a contaminated source, or items wet for several days, usually means discarding.

Drying a mattress safely

Lift the mattress off the floor immediately. Blot, do not rub, and press out as much water as you can with towels. Stand it on its side in a well-ventilated room, with a fan and a dehumidifier running, and keep it off the carpet. A mattress that is soaked through is difficult to dry, because foam and inner springs trap water.

If a smell returns after it feels dry, the inside is still wet. Mattresses that hold water can grow mold, so when in doubt, replace the item and photograph it first for your insurer.

Sofas and upholstered furniture

Move the furniture out of the wet area. Remove cushions and stand them on edge. Run fans and a dehumidifier. Foam and padding hold water for a long time, so a surface that feels dry can still be wet inside. Professional drying equipment and moisture meters can check the inside, which a hand cannot.

If the furniture was in contact with floodwater or sewage, throw it away. Our sewage backup cleanup page explains why porous items that touched contaminated water are not worth saving.

Document before you discard

If you may file a claim, photograph each item, write down the brand, age and rough value and keep a few identifying pieces such as the tag. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises documenting damage before cleanup and keeping receipts. Do not throw anything out before you have a record.

Renters should also tell the landlord and their renters insurer. If the leak came from above, the ceiling needs attention too, and ceiling water damage repair explains how that is handled.

How to tell what can be saved

The category of water matters most. Clean water from a supply line gives you a chance to dry and save some items. Water from a sewer backup or a flood that touched the ground should be treated as contaminated, and porous items such as mattresses and upholstery are usually thrown away.

Foam and padding hold water deep inside, so surface drying rarely reaches the core. If an item stayed wet for more than a day or two, expect to replace it.

Steps for items that may be saved

Move the item out of the wet room, stand it on edge or on a rack and let air circulate. Use fans and a dehumidifier. Remove cushion covers if they come off and wash them according to the label. Press a towel firmly into the fabric to pull out water, then repeat.

Keep photos and a list before throwing anything away, since your insurer may ask for them.

Be careful with electronics and appliances

Do not plug in a wet television, lamp or power strip. Let items dry fully and ask a repair shop to check them first. Recliners and sofa beds with motors or wiring can be unsafe if moisture got in.

When in doubt, replace it rather than risk a fire.

Disposing of furniture in Johnstown

Ask the City of Johnstown or your trash hauler about rules for bulky items such as mattresses and sofas before you set them at the curb, since many places have special pickup rules. City Hall can be reached at (814) 539-2504. A wet mattress is heavy and hard to move, so plan ahead.

If you live outside the city, in Seward or another nearby borough, your borough or hauler sets the rules. Wrap wet items in plastic to keep them from dripping, and keep them away from children and pets until they are gone.

The same applies in Elton, where there is no borough office to ask.

Preventing mold on soft goods

Soft materials need to dry quickly. A mattress or sofa that stays damp for more than a day or two is at risk. Keep the room cool and dry, run a dehumidifier and open a window if the weather allows. Do not store wet items in a closed room or a garage with no airflow.

If you see visible growth on furniture, throw it out rather than trying to clean it. For the structure itself, drying with meters is the safer path.

Standards and sources

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.
