What a Sudden Jump in Your Water Bill Can Mean in Johnstown, PA

Seven places to look for a hidden leak and how to use your meter to confirm it.

☎ (814) 358-8169

By the Johnstown Water Damage Pros team

A higher bill is often the first clue

People often discover a hidden leak when the water bill arrives. A jump with no change in how you use water suggests something is wrong. It might be a running toilet, a leaking pipe, a failed fixture or a line under the floor. It can also be a billing or meter issue, so check before you assume.

Do not ignore a spike. A leak that is large enough to move a bill can also be soaking a wall or floor you cannot see.

Read your meter to confirm

Turn off every tap and appliance that uses water, including ice makers and irrigation. Find the water meter and look at the small flow indicator or the dial. If it moves while nothing is on, water is escaping somewhere. Write down the reading, wait an hour with no water use and read again. A change confirms a leak.

If the meter does not move, the extra water may have been used, or the bill may include other charges. Look at the bill carefully, including the billing period and the sewer charges.

What if the leak is inside a wall?

A leak in a wall often announces itself through a stain, a warm spot, a musty smell or the sound of running water. A moisture meter and thermal camera can find where it is without opening the wall. Once the pipe is fixed, the wall should be dried before it is closed up.

If there is water damage, the restoration process starts with moisture mapping and drying. Ask your insurer about coverage, since leaks that went on for months are treated differently from sudden failures.

A ten minute leak test

1

Turn everything off

Stop the washer, dishwasher and ice maker and make sure nobody is using water.

2

Read the meter

Write down the meter reading and look at the small dial or leak indicator.

3

Wait and look again

Wait thirty minutes with no water use and read it again.

4

Compare

If the number moved or the indicator spins, water is going somewhere.

Common culprits

A running toilet is the most common cause of a high bill. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and see if color shows up in the bowl without flushing. Dripping faucets, a stuck irrigation valve and a failing water heater relief valve are other usual suspects.

A leak in the line between the meter and the house can run for weeks without showing indoors. Wet spots in the yard or a sound of running water are clues.

Talk to the provider and your insurer

Ask your water provider whether it offers any adjustment after a leak is repaired. Policies differ, so ask what documents they want, such as a plumber invoice. Keep the bill and the repair record together.

If the leak caused damage inside the house, call your insurer and a restoration company about the damage. See burst pipe water damage for what to do when a line has failed.

Prevent the next spike

Check toilet flappers once a year, replace old hoses and know where the main valve is. Some homeowners add a smart shutoff valve or a leak sensor near the water heater and washer. These cost little compared with a flooded floor.

Compare each bill with the previous year so you notice a gradual rise early.

Water and sewer service in Johnstown

The Greater Johnstown Water Authority manages the sanitary sewer system in the City of Johnstown and can be reached at 814-533-4300. That is the number to call about your meter, your account and questions on how sewer charges appear on your bill.

If you live outside the city, in a place like Elton, your water may come from a different provider or a private well, so check your bill to find out who to call. For a leak that has already damaged the floor, see hardwood floor water damage repair.

Customers in Salix should check which provider sends their bill.

Who to call

Call your water provider for questions about the meter and the bill. Call a plumber to find and fix the leak. Call a restoration company if water has damaged the structure or finishes. Keep the old bills, since they show when the usage started to rise.

Ask the water provider whether it offers any adjustment for leaks, since policies vary. Do not assume one exists, and ask what documentation is needed.
