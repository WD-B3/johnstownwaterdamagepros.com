Sewage backup: what to do first

Sewage backup cleanup is the removal and sanitizing of contaminated water that has come up through a floor drain, toilet, tub or sink. The 24/7 line in Johnstown, PA answers these calls every day of the year. Stay out of the water, keep children and pets away, stop using sinks, toilets and washing machines so no more water goes down the line, and call us.

Do not try to wash it down a drain or clean it with a household mop. Contaminated water spreads on anything you use to move it.

Safety first

If water has reached outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, do not step in it. Call 911 or the utility before you call us. Turn off power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water.

Sewage carries bacteria and other contaminants. Questions about health effects belong to your physician, and a crew handles the cleanup in boots, gloves and suits so you do not have to.

When sewage backup is an emergency

It is always an emergency. Contaminated water keeps soaking carpet, drywall and insulation, and the odor and risk grow with time. Backups often show up at night or during heavy rain, which is why the line is staffed after hours and on weekends, with same-day response when crews are available.

If you see sewage in one fixture, check the others. A backup in several drains at once suggests the main line or a municipal problem, and a backup in only the lowest drain suggests a clog in your own lateral.

Why the water is different

Most homeowners search for symptoms, such as water coming up through the floor drain or a toilet that gurgles and overflows. The trade calls it Category 3 water, meaning it is grossly contaminated. Everything porous it touches, like carpet, pad, insulation and drywall, is generally removed and discarded rather than dried, because moisture alone does not make those materials safe again.

Hard surfaces such as concrete and sealed tile can be cleaned and disinfected. This is why this page and water extraction are separate: the equipment is similar, but the safety steps are not.

How the cleanup is done

The crew wears protective equipment, extracts the water and disposes of contaminated materials. Walls and floors that stay are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial agent, and the crew checks moisture levels before drying begins with air movers and dehumidifiers. The affected area is usually sealed off to keep contamination away from the rest of the house.

A written scope lists what is removed and what is cleaned. Photos before removal help your insurer, and it is worth keeping a short list of items that were discarded.

Sewer backups in the Johnstown area

Johnstown is an old city, and older sewer systems can surcharge during heavy rain. We do not have a local figure for how often that happens, so ask the Authority about your street. Basement drains are the lowest point in the system and the first to back up.

Ask your plumber about a backwater valve on the lateral, and ask your insurer about sewer backup coverage, since a standard policy may not include it. For outlying areas such as Nanty Glo, sewer service may come from a different authority, so confirm who to call.

Safety checklist and standards

The drying and water category terms used here come from ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry standard for professional water damage restoration. For claims, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing damage before cleanup and keeping receipts.

  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Treat sewage or flood water as contaminated and wear gloves and boots.
  • Leave the home and call 911 or the gas utility if you smell gas.
  • Wash hands before eating after any contact with wet materials.
